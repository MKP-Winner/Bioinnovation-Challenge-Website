What is Alzheimer’s?

Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive brain disorder that affects memory, thinking, and behavior. Researchers study biological, environmental, and genetic factors because no single factor explains every case.

Important: This website is an educational project. Genetic information can be associated with risk or inherited disease, but this site does not diagnose Alzheimer’s or predict an individual’s future.
🧠

Brain changes

Alzheimer’s is associated with biological changes including amyloid plaques and tau-related changes, alongside progressive loss of healthy neurons and connections.

💭

Common symptoms

Early symptoms can include worsening memory, difficulty with planning or problem-solving, and changes that interfere with everyday activities. Symptoms vary between people.

🔬

Why research genetics?

Genetic research helps scientists understand biological pathways, identify risk factors, and study rare inherited forms of Alzheimer’s.

Research framework

Multiple pieces make the picture.

Genes are one part of Alzheimer’s research. Age, family history, vascular health, environment, and other factors also matter.

1

Genetic background

Some variants raise or lower risk; a small number of rare variants can cause inherited forms of disease.

2

Biological pathways

Researchers examine processes involving amyloid, tau, lipids, inflammation, synapses, and immune-cell function.

3

Clinical evidence

Real-world assessment can include medical history, cognitive evaluation, imaging, and other tests. No single gene lookup replaces clinical assessment.
